# 50 Kitchen Verbs with Pictures

## INTRO
Welcome to Everyday English with Pictures. Today we are in the kitchen, and we will learn fifty verbs for cooking and preparing food.
Here is how it works. First you see a picture: try to guess the word. Then listen, and say the word out loud. You will also hear a short question and answer, so you know how to use it in a real sentence.
Every ten words there is a quick review, and at the end there is a final test. Get a pen and paper ready, and keep your score.

## WORDS | Getting food ready
Let's start with the jobs we do before cooking.
@ word wash | 🚰 🥦 | to clean something with water | Did you wash the vegetables? | Yes, I washed them under the tap.
@ word rinse | 🍚 💧 | to wash something quickly in clean water | Why are you rinsing the rice? | Rinsing it removes the extra starch.
@ word peel | 🍌 | to take the skin off a fruit or vegetable | Can you peel the potatoes for me? | Sure, where is the peeler?
@ word chop | 🔪 🥕 | to cut food into pieces with a knife | What are you doing with the onions? | I'm chopping them for the curry.
@ word slice | 🍞 🔪 | to cut food into thin, flat pieces | How many pieces of bread should I slice? | Four slices, please.
@ word dice | 🥒 🎲 | to cut food into small squares, like little cubes | How small should I dice the cucumber? | About the size of a pea.
Chop, slice and dice are all ways to cut. Chop means pieces of any shape. Slice means thin and flat. Dice means small cubes.
@ word grate | 🧀 | to rub food on a grater to make very small pieces | Do you want me to grate the cheese? | Yes, just a little on top.
@ word mash | 🥔 🍴 | to press cooked food until it is soft and smooth | How do you make the filling? | I mash boiled potatoes with spices.
@ word squeeze | 🍋 | to press something hard to get the juice out | Should I squeeze some lemon on the salad? | Yes, half a lemon is enough.
@ word crack | 🥚 | to break an egg open | How many eggs did you crack? | Three, for the omelette.

## REVIEW | Review 1: name the pictures
@ review peel, chop, grate, squeeze, slice, mash, crack, rinse
Look at the eight pictures and say each word before the timer ends.
If you got six or more, you are doing really well. If you are enjoying this, please subscribe: a new picture lesson comes every week.

## WORDS | Measuring and mixing
Now let's measure and mix.
@ word measure | 🥄 🥛 | to find the exact amount of something | How much milk do we need? | Measure one cup.
@ word weigh | ⚖️ | to find how heavy something is | Can you weigh the flour? | It's two hundred grams.
@ word pour | 🫗 | to make a liquid flow out of a container | Shall I pour the tea? | Yes, please, not too full.
@ word stir | 🥄 🍲 | to move a spoon around and around in food or a drink | Why do you keep stirring the dal? | So it doesn't stick to the pan.
@ word mix | 🥣 | to put things together so they become one | What do you mix with the flour? | Water and a pinch of salt.
@ word whisk | 🥚 🥣 | to mix very fast with a fork or a whisk, to add air | How do you make the eggs fluffy? | I whisk them for a minute.
@ word blend | 🥤 🍓 | to mix food in a machine until it is smooth | What's in your smoothie? | I blended a banana with some milk.
@ word knead | 🫓 🤲 | to press and fold dough with your hands | How long do you knead the dough? | About five minutes, until it is soft.
@ word roll | 🫓 | to make dough flat and round with a rolling pin | Can you roll the chapatis? | Sure, I'll make them nice and round.
@ word spread | 🧈 🍞 | to put a thin layer of something soft on food | What do you spread on your toast? | Butter, and sometimes jam.

## REVIEW | Review 2: name the pictures
@ review weigh, pour, stir, whisk, blend, knead, roll, spread
Eight more pictures. Say each word out loud.

## WORDS | Cooking with heat
Time to switch on the stove. These verbs are all about heat.
@ word heat | 🔥 🍳 | to make something hot | Is the oil ready? | Not yet, I'm still heating it.
@ word boil | 🫖 💧 | to heat water until it bubbles | Can you boil some water for tea? | Sure, I'll put the kettle on.
@ word simmer | 🍲 | to cook gently, with small bubbles, just below boiling | Is the soup done? | Let it simmer for ten more minutes.
Boil means big, fast bubbles. Simmer means small, slow bubbles.
@ word fry | 🍳 | to cook food in hot oil | How do you like your eggs? | Fried, please, with toast.
@ word bake | 🍰 | to cook food in an oven, like bread and cakes | What are you baking? | A chocolate cake for Sunday.
@ word roast | 🍗 | to cook meat or vegetables in an oven or over a fire | Shall we roast the vegetables? | Yes, with a little olive oil.
@ word grill | 🌽 🔥 | to cook food over or under strong, direct heat | Where are you going to grill the corn? | On the barbecue in the garden.
@ word steam | 🥟 | to cook food with the hot steam from boiling water | How do you cook momos? | We steam them for ten minutes.
@ word toast | 🍞 🔥 | to make bread brown and crispy with heat | Do you want me to toast your bread? | Yes, but not too dark.
@ word melt | 🧈 🔥 | to make something solid turn into liquid with heat | Why are you heating the butter? | I need to melt it for the cake.

## REVIEW | Review 3: name the pictures
@ review boil, simmer, fry, bake, roast, steam, toast, melt
Here's your third review. Say all eight.

## WORDS | Finishing touches
The food is almost ready. Let's add the finishing touches.
@ word season | 🧂 | to add salt, pepper or spices to give food flavour | Did you season the chicken? | Yes, with salt, pepper and garlic.
@ word sprinkle | 🧂 🥗 | to drop small bits of something over food | What do you sprinkle on top? | Fresh coriander and a little chilli.
@ word taste | 👅 🥄 | to try a little food to see what it is like | Can you taste the sauce? | Mmm, it needs more salt.
@ word flip | 🥞 | to turn food over quickly in the pan | When do you flip the pancake? | When you see small bubbles on top.
@ word drain | 🍝 💧 | to pour away the water from cooked food | Is the pasta ready? | Yes, I'm just draining it.
@ word scoop | 🍨 🥄 | to take out food with a spoon or a curved tool | How much ice cream do you want? | Two scoops, please.
@ word cover | 🍲 | to put a lid or something on top of food | Should I cover the rice? | Yes, keep the lid on so it stays warm.
@ word wrap | 🌯 | to put paper or foil around food | Can you wrap these sandwiches for lunch? | Sure, I'll use some foil.
@ word cool | 🥧 💨 | to let hot food become less hot | Can I eat the pie now? | Let it cool for a few minutes first.
@ word freeze | 🧊 ❄️ | to make food very cold, so it becomes hard | What do you do with leftover soup? | I freeze it for next week.

## REVIEW | Review 4: name the pictures
@ review season, sprinkle, taste, flip, drain, scoop, wrap, freeze
Fourth review. You know what to do.

## WORDS | Serving and cleaning up
Finally, let's eat, and then clean up.
@ word reheat | ♨️ 🍛 | to make cooked food hot again | Is this curry from yesterday? | Yes, I just reheated it.
@ word set the table | 🍽️ | to put plates, glasses and cutlery on the table | Can you set the table, please? | Sure, for how many people?
@ word serve | 🍛 🍽️ | to put food on plates and give it to people | When will you serve dinner? | In about fifteen minutes.
@ word pass | 🧂 🤝 | to give something to a person near you | Could you pass the salt, please? | Here you go.
@ word wipe | 🧽 | to clean a surface with a cloth | Who wiped the table? | I did, it's clean now.
@ word scrub | 🧽 🍳 | to clean something by rubbing it hard | Is the pan clean? | Not yet, I need to scrub it.
@ word dry | 🍽️ 🧻 | to take the water off something | Shall I dry the plates? | Yes, the towel is on the hook.
@ word load | 🍽️ 📥 | to put things into a machine, like a dishwasher | Did you load the dishwasher? | Yes, and I switched it on.
@ word put away | 🗄️ | to put things back in their place | Where should I put away the cups? | In the cupboard, top shelf.
@ word take out the rubbish | 🗑️ | to carry the rubbish bag outside | Whose turn is it to take out the rubbish? | It's mine, I'll do it now.
In American English, people say trash, or garbage, instead of rubbish.

## REVIEW | Review 5: name the pictures

Last review before the final test.
