# Can, Could, Should, Must

## INTRO
Welcome to Verb Forms and Grammar with Pictures. This is the last lesson of the season: modal verbs. Can, could, should and must.
These small verbs change the meaning of a sentence: is it possible, is it polite, is it a good idea, or is it a rule?
First, three rules that every modal verb follows.

## STUDY | Three rules for every modal
@ point Modal + base verb, **no to**: She can **swim**. Not, she can to swim.
@ point **No s** with he, she, it: He **can** drive. Not, he cans.
@ point Questions and negatives without do: **Can you** help me? I **can't** come.
Easy! Modals do the work of do and does by themselves.

## STUDY | Can and could
@ pic 🏊 | **can** = ability now: I **can** swim. I **can't** play the piano.
Can is for things you are able to do.
@ pic 🚲 | **could** = ability in the past: I **could** ride a bike when I was five.
Could is the past of can.
@ pic 🙏 | **Can** I / **Could** you...? = requests: **Could you** open the window, please?
For requests, could is a little more polite than can.
@ pic 🔓 | **can** = permission: You **can** use my laptop. You **can't** park here.

## STUDY | Should: advice
@ pic 💡 | **should** = a good idea: You **should** see a doctor. You **shouldn't** eat so late.
Should gives advice. It's a suggestion, not a rule.
@ point Asking for advice: What **should** I do? **Should** I call her?

## STUDY | Must and have to: rules
@ pic ⚠️ | **must** = a strong rule or a strong feeling: You **must** wear a seatbelt. I **must** call my mum!
@ pic 📋 | **have to** = a rule from outside: I **have to** wear a uniform at work.
In everyday speech, have to is more common than must.
@ table | Meaning | Example ; mustn't | it's not allowed! | You mustn't smoke here. ; don't have to | it's not necessary | You don't have to come. It's optional.
Be careful: these two look similar, but the meanings are very different.

## PRACTICE | Choose the modal
@ q [🏊] I ___ swim when I was six. | can | *could | should
Could: ability in the past.
@ q [🙏] Which request is the most polite? | Open the window. | Can you open the window? | *Could you open the window, please?
Could you, please, is the most polite.
@ q [🤒] You look ill. You ___ go home. | *should | could | must
Should: advice.
@ q [🎟️] The concert is free. You ___ buy a ticket. | mustn't | *don't have to | can't
Don't have to: it's not necessary. Mustn't would mean it's not allowed.
@ q [🚭] You ___ smoke in the hospital. | don't have to | *mustn't | shouldn't to
Mustn't: it's not allowed.
@ q [🧑‍💼] At my job, I ___ start at eight. | *have to | should | could
Have to: a rule from your work.
@ q [🎹] ___ you play the guitar? | Do | *Can | Must
Can you play? Asking about ability.
@ q [🌧️] It's raining. You ___ take an umbrella. | *should | mustn't | can't
Should: good advice.

## PRACTICE | Spot the mistake
@ fix She can to swim very well. => She can swim very well.
No to after a modal.
@ fix He musts finish the report today. => He must finish the report today.
No s on modals.
@ fix Do you can help me? => Can you help me?
No do with modals.
@ fix You should to drink more water. => You should drink more water.
No to after should.
