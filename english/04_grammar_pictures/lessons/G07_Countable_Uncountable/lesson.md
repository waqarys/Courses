# Countable & Uncountable Nouns

## INTRO
Welcome to Verb Forms and Grammar with Pictures. Today: countable and uncountable nouns.
Some things you can count: one apple, two apples. Some things you can't: you don't say two rices. This changes the words we use with them.
Let's see some pictures.

## STUDY | Can you count it?
@ pic 🍎 🍎 🍎 | **Countable**: one apple, two apples, three apples. They have a plural.
Countable nouns can be singular or plural. In the singular, they need a or an.
@ pic 🍚 | **Uncountable**: rice, water, money, bread. No plural, no a or an.
Uncountable nouns are often liquids, materials, food in small pieces, and ideas.

## WORDS | Countable or uncountable?
@ word an apple | 🍎 | countable: one apple, two apples | How many apples do we need? | Six, for the cake.
@ word rice | 🍚 | uncountable: some rice, a bag of rice | How much rice should I cook? | One cup is enough.
@ word a chair | 🪑 | countable: one chair, four chairs | How many chairs are there? | There are four chairs.
@ word water | 💧 | uncountable: some water, a glass of water | Can I have some water? | Sure, here's a glass.
@ word a coin | 🪙 | countable: one coin, ten coins | Have you got any coins for the machine? | Only two.
@ word money | 💵 | uncountable: some money, a lot of money | How much money do you have? | Not much!
@ word a sandwich | 🥪 | countable: one sandwich, two sandwiches | Would you like a sandwich? | Yes, please. I'm starving.
@ word bread | 🍞 | uncountable: some bread, a loaf of bread | Is there any bread? | Yes, there's half a loaf.
@ word a suitcase | 🧳 | countable: a suitcase, two suitcases | How many suitcases are you taking? | Just one.
@ word luggage | 🛄 | uncountable: some luggage, a piece of luggage | Do you have much luggage? | Just one suitcase.
@ word a song | 🎵 | countable: a song, three songs | What's your favourite song? | Anything by Coldplay.
@ word music | 🎶 | uncountable: some music, a piece of music | What music do you like? | Mostly jazz.
@ word a job | 💼 | countable: a job, two jobs | Has she got a new job? | Yes, at a bank.
@ word work | 🧑‍💻 | uncountable: a lot of work | Do you have much work today? | Tons. I'll be late.
@ word a suggestion | 💡 | countable: a suggestion, some suggestions | Can I make a suggestion? | Of course.
@ word advice | 🗣️ | uncountable: some advice, a piece of advice | Can you give me some advice? | Sure. Sleep on it.

## STUDY | The words that go with them
@ table | Countable | Uncountable ; a / an | a coin | — ; some / any | some coins | some money ; how ...? | how many coins? | how much money? ; small amount | a few coins | a little money ; big amount | a lot of coins | a lot of money
Use many and a few with countable nouns, much and a little with uncountable nouns. Some, any and a lot of work with both.
@ point To count uncountable things, use a container or a piece: **a glass of** water, **a loaf of** bread, **a piece of** advice.

## PRACTICE | Much or many? A few or a little?
@ q How ___ people came to the party? | much | *many | a little
Many: people are countable.
@ q How ___ milk do we need? | *much | many | a few
Much: milk is uncountable.
@ q I've got ___ questions for you. | a little | *a few | much
A few: questions are countable.
@ q Is there ___ sugar in this tea? | many | a few | *any
Any, in questions and negatives.
@ q We don't have ___ time. Hurry up! | many | *much | a few
Much: time is uncountable here.
@ q Add ___ salt to the soup. | a few | *a little | many
A little: salt is uncountable.
@ q There are ___ good restaurants near here. | much | *a lot of | a little
A lot of works with both, and it's natural here.
@ q Can I have ___ glass of water? | some | *a | much
A glass: the glass is countable.

## PRACTICE | Common mistakes
@ fix Can you give me an advice? => Can you give me some advice?
Advice is uncountable. Or say, a piece of advice.
@ fix I need to buy some furnitures. => I need to buy some furniture.
Furniture is uncountable: no s.
@ fix How many money do you have? => How much money do you have?
Money is uncountable.
@ fix The informations are on the website. => The information is on the website.
Information is uncountable: no s, and a singular verb.

## OUTRO
Great work. Remember: if you can't put a number in front of it, it's probably uncountable.
