# -ed Endings: /t/, /d/ or /id/

## INTRO
Welcome to Verb Forms and Grammar with Pictures. Today: how to say the e d at the end of regular verbs in the past.
We write e d every time, but we say it in three different ways. Listen: walked. Played. Wanted.
The rule is about the last sound of the verb, not the last letter. Let's see.

## STUDY | One spelling, three sounds
@ table Sound | When? | Examples ; /t/ | after p, k, f, s, sh, ch, x | walked, watched, laughed ; /d/ | after vowels and other voiced sounds | played, cleaned, lived ; /id/ | after t and d only | wanted, needed, decided
@ point **/t/**: walk**ed** sounds like walkt. One syllable.
After a quiet, voiceless sound, like p, k, s or sh, the ending is a t sound.
@ point **/d/**: play**ed** sounds like playd. One syllable.
After a vowel or a voiced sound, like l, n, m or v, the ending is a d sound.
@ point **/id/**: want**ed** sounds like want-id. One extra syllable!
Only after t or d do we add an extra syllable. This is the most common mistake: don't say walk-id or play-id.

## QUIZ | Which sound?
